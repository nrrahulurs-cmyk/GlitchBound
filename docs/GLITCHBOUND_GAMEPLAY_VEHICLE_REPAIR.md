## Startup and initialization path

1. `index.html` loads `/src/main.js` as the browser module entry.
2. `src/main.js` creates the renderer, scene, city (`createCity`), procedural city-life (`createCityLife`) and atmosphere.
3. `GLTFLoader` loads the required project models from `/assets/<name>.glb` with one `Promise.all`. The model list is `interceptor`, `drone`, `crate`, `kiosk`, `mercenary`, `av`, `pulse-carbine`, `ion-bolt`, `muzzle-flash` and `impact-shards`.
4. The existing interceptor root is cloned for the player placement and separately for the parked placement. The player visual follows `carPosition` in `updateCamera`; the parked visual is handed to `simulation.streetPhysics` as a physical, non-drivable prop.
5. A separate kinematic Cannon box is the player vehicle body. `movePlayer` owns throttle, reverse, steering, collision and accepted movement. `interact` enters/exits only the player vehicle at `carPosition`; `restart` restores that one vehicle and resets the street simulation.
6. `animate` advances movement/physics, city life, camera, weapons and HUD. `window.__glitchbound.snapshot()` exposes runtime state after initialization; `window.__neon` is retained as the compatibility alias.

If any required existing GLB rejects in the `Promise.all`, `init` catches the error, sets the visible initialization failure state and displays a GLITCHBOUND error toast. There is no visual fallback model that could be concurrently rendered with a replacement.

## Confirmed vehicle architecture and root causes

### Why the old vehicle appearance remains

The source has no Tripo car path or replacement configuration. `src/main.js` directly loads `interceptor.glb`, creates the player render object from that asset, and clones the same asset for `VEHICLE-PARKED`. `src/asset-placements.js` identifies the player at `(-3.8, 0.065, 23.5)` and the parked car at `(-9.2, 0.065, -74)`. Thus the old interceptor remains because neither Tripo export exists in the checked project nor is referenced by the loader. This is a missing-asset/missing-mapping issue, not evidence of an extra Tripo model loader.

The two specific desired paths from the existing custom-asset map are absent:

- `public/assets/tripo/car-01-990-cyan-magenta.glb`
- `public/assets/tripo/car-02-armored-blue.glb`

The repository, `Downloads`, `OneDrive\Documents`, and `Desktop` search found the existing project models and Blender files, but no corresponding custom vehicle `.glb`, `.gltf`, `.fbx`, `.obj`, or `.stl` exports. No screenshots or video previews were treated as geometry.

### The game does not contain two drivable vehicle slots

There is one `VEHICLE-PLAYER` car body/controller and one `VEHICLE-PARKED` physical prop. The parked prop cannot be entered: `interact` checks distance to the sole `carPosition`. Its collider and damage visual are independent from the player's kinematic body. The game does not currently have a second drivable spawn, car-selection flow, or second player controller. Assigning Car B to the parked vehicle, patrol aircraft, or traffic would change its role by guesswork.

City traffic is a separate intentional system: `src/city-life.js` constructs route vehicles from procedural box parts (`createTrafficVehicle`) and updates them as instanced meshes. It is neither a hidden load of `interceptor.glb` nor one of the two player-controlled vehicle slots. Traffic must remain intact.

### Existing vehicle model/physics contract

- `public/assets/interceptor.glb`, generated by `blender/build_assets.py`, is the existing road car; `blender/asset-manifest.json` records bounds about `2.895 × 1.364 × 5.278 m`, 9,280 vertices, 6,896 faces, and 13 materials. It is a combined model without named wheel pivots or animation clips.
- `blender/asset-manifest.json` and `blender/README.md` document Blender Z-up/+Y-forward to glTF Y-up/-Z-forward conversion. The current runtime's road-forward convention is `(-sin(yaw), -cos(yaw))` and matches this axis.
- The player's collision body is a separate kinematic `CANNON.Box` with half-extents `(1.4, 0.64, 2.60) m`; vehicle movement, damage, lights, audio, and entry/exit rely on this existing entity. A replacement must change its render mesh only until compatibility is measured.
- The parked collider belongs to `src/street-physics.js` and `src/simulation.js`; the player body and parked prop have distinct roles and must not be removed together.
- `src/city-life.js` owns procedural traffic layout, route motion, physical collision and reset. `src/simulation.js` owns interactions with traffic and damage/crime outcomes.
